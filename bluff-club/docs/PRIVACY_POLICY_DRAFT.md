## Summary

Bluff Club is a party game played on one phone. Gameplay works offline. The things
you type or see while playing (player names, secret words, roles and votes) stay on
your device. We do not run our own servers, accounts, chat, advertising or analytics
services.

## Information stored on your device

- **Game data:** player names you enter, game settings, session scores, word history
  per pack, and any round in progress. Stored in the app's private storage so a game
  can survive the app being closed.
- **Local playtest counters:** simple totals (for example, rounds finished, purchases
  attempted). No names, words, roles, votes or free text.

This data is not sent to us. You can delete it with **Settings → Reset**, or by
uninstalling the app. Android's backup feature may include app data in your
personal Google backup if you have backup turned on; that is controlled by your
device settings. [Owner: confirm the final backup configuration.]

If you choose **Copy stats to share with the developers**, the local counters are
copied to your clipboard. Nothing is sent unless you paste and send them yourself.

## Purchases

Optional packs are bought through **Google Play**. To confirm and restore purchases,
the app uses **RevenueCat** (RevenueCat, Inc.) as a service provider. When you open the
store or buy or restore packs, the following are processed by Google and RevenueCat
to deliver and verify purchases:

- an anonymous, randomly generated app user ID (we do not ask for your name or email)
- purchase records (product, time, order/transaction identifiers, purchase status)
- technical data needed to operate the service, as described in RevenueCat's own
  privacy documentation. [Owner: list exactly what RevenueCat's current SDK collects,
  e.g. device/app version and store country, from RevenueCat's docs at release.]

Google processes payments under Google's privacy policy. We never receive your card
or bank details. Links: [Google Privacy Policy], [RevenueCat Privacy Policy].

## Security

The app blocks screenshots and recent-app previews during rounds where Android allows
it. This cannot stop someone photographing the screen, and on-device data is not
protected against rooted or modified devices.
